SOC 2, explained

What "SOC 2 Type II certified" actually means.

SOC 2 is an independent audit of a service company's controls, defined by the American Institute of CPAs. A licensed CPA firm examines how we protect customer data and issues a report. It is the standard insurers and their procurement teams ask for before sharing data with a vendor.

Type I vs. Type II

A Type I report checks that controls are designed properly on a single day. A Type II report, which we hold, checks that the controls actually operated effectively over a period of months. The auditor tests evidence from that whole period, not a snapshot.

Why it matters to you

You do not have to take our word for it. An independent auditor tested how we handle access, encryption, monitoring, vendors and incidents, and wrote down what they found. Carriers and partners can read that report under NDA.

The five Trust Services Criteria an auditor examines

01Security+

What the criteria cover

Protection against unauthorized access, both physical and logical. This is the one criterion every SOC 2 report must include.

What we do

  • Multi-factor authentication for every employee and contractor
  • Role-based access with least privilege, reviewed quarterly
  • Encryption in transit (TLS) and at rest
  • Vulnerability scanning and third-party penetration testing
  • Centralized logging and alerting on suspicious activity
02Availability+

What the criteria cover

The system is available for operation and use as committed. Audits run on deadlines, and so do we.

What we do

  • Cloud infrastructure with redundancy across availability zones
  • Encrypted backups, tested restores
  • Monitoring with on-call response
  • A documented business continuity and disaster recovery plan
03Processing integrity+

What the criteria cover

Processing is complete, valid, accurate, timely and authorized. For an audit platform, this is the heart of trust.

What we do

  • Every Penny result records its inputs, rule version and model version, so it can be reproduced exactly
  • Every class code and payroll figure links to the source document and the rule applied
  • Auditor review and approval before any audit is final
  • Change management and testing before releases
04Confidentiality+

What the criteria cover

Information designated as confidential is protected as agreed. Payroll and premium are confidential by default.

What we do

  • Each carrier's data is isolated; nothing is pooled across carriers except learned correction patterns with no payroll, premium or policyholder names
  • Confidentiality agreements with every employee, contractor and vendor
  • Data classification and handling rules for payroll, tax and policy records
  • Secure deletion at the end of the agreed retention period
05Privacy+

What the criteria cover

Personal information is collected, used, retained, disclosed and disposed of in line with our commitments and the law.

What we do

  • We collect only what the audit needs and tell insureds what that is
  • Employee names and pay are used for classification and premium only
  • Social Security numbers that appear in uploaded records are redacted where they are not needed
  • Insureds and carriers can request access to or deletion of their data
How data moves

Three parties, one set of records, clear walls.

A business shares records with Penny. Penny prepares the audit for the insurer. The insurer's auditor makes the decision. Each party sees what it needs and nothing more.

Who we protect

Three groups, three sets of commitments.

Businesses

  • We ask only for what the audit needs, and we tell you why we need it
  • Your records are used for your audit, never sold or shared with other businesses
  • You can see every document you uploaded and request deletion when the audit is closed
  • The free tools run without an account and do not keep what you type

Employees

  • Names and pay are used only to classify work and calculate premium
  • Social Security numbers are redacted from uploaded records where they are not needed
  • Employees are never contacted by Penny; the business and its bookkeeper are
  • Employee records are deleted on the same schedule as the audit file

Insurers

  • Your book is isolated from every other carrier's
  • Single sign-on, role-based access and a full audit trail for your team
  • Data processing terms built to support your obligations under state insurance data security laws
  • The SOC 2 Type II report, penetration test summary and security questionnaire are available under NDA

AI and your data

How the AI uses what you share.

What the AI reads

Payroll registers, tax filings, certificates and policy documents, to extract figures and propose classifications. Each extraction links back to the page it came from.

What the AI never does

It never finalizes an audit on its own, never shares one carrier's data with another, and never trains outside providers' models. Our AI providers are bound by agreements that prohibit training on customer data.

What improves the model

Auditor-confirmed outcomes. A business's claim or a Penny suggestion alone never becomes training data. Pooled learning uses correction patterns only, with no payroll, premium or names.

What you can check

Any result can be re-run with the same inputs, rule version and model version to confirm it matches. Regulators and auditors can inspect the record of every decision.

Questions we get

Straight answers.

Is Penny itself SOC 2 certified, or just Propono?+

Penny runs on the Propono platform, and the SOC 2 Type II report covers that platform, its infrastructure and the people who operate it. Penny's free tools, Pro accounts and the insurer dispute portal all run inside that boundary.

Can my carrier see what I asked Penny?+

If you are using Penny inside your carrier's audit portal, your questions and uploads are part of your audit file with that carrier. If you are using Penny on your own, your data is yours and is not shared with a carrier unless you choose to send it.

Where is data stored?+

In United States cloud regions, encrypted at rest. We do not move customer data outside the United States.

What happens if there is a security incident?+

We follow a documented incident response plan: contain, investigate, notify affected customers within the timelines in our agreements and applicable law, and publish what we learned.

How do I get the SOC 2 report?+

Ask. Carriers, audit firms and partners receive the full report, penetration test summary and completed security questionnaire under a standard NDA.

Need the details for your security review?

We will send the SOC 2 Type II report, penetration test summary and a completed security questionnaire under NDA, and get on a call with your security team.
